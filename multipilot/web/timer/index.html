A free FPV lap timer for practice and racing

This page turns a phone or laptop into a 5.8 GHz FPV lap timer. An ESP32-C5 running the free C5MK firmware measures each quad's video signal about 1000 times a second, and the page records a lap every time a quad passes the gate. There's no app to install, no account, and no Raspberry Pi or race server to set up.

Features

  • Practice and race modes for 1–4 pilots, each on their own video channel
  • Raceband, Fatshark, Boscam A, B and E, and Lowband channels from 5180 to 5917 MHz
  • Spoken lap times, with last and best lap for every pilot
  • Automatic noise-floor tracking and Low, Medium and High sensitivity presets
  • A live RSSI graph with draggable entry and exit thresholds for fine tuning
  • Results you can export as CSV

What you need

  • An ESP32-C5 board with a 5 GHz antenna, such as the Waveshare ESP32-C5-Zero or Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32-C5
  • C5MK firmware, flashed from your browser with the C5 flasher
  • Chrome or Edge on a computer, or Chrome on an Android phone
  • A USB data cable

Questions

Do I need RotorHazard or a Raspberry Pi?

No. The receiver plugs straight into your phone or computer and the timer runs in the browser. If you already race with RotorHazard, the same receiver can also plug into RotorHazard as eight nodes.

How many pilots can it time?

The browser timer handles 1–4 pilots. One C5MK receiver can measure up to eight, which you can use through RotorHazard or an FPVGate timer.

Does it work on an iPhone or iPad?

No. Safari and other iOS browsers can't talk to USB devices. Use Chrome on Android, or Chrome or Edge on a computer.

How accurate are the lap times?

Each signal reading is timestamped by the receiver's own clock, so delays in the USB link or the phone don't move the lap times. Lap accuracy against video hasn't been measured yet.

Is anything uploaded?

No. The timer runs entirely in your browser. Settings stay on your device, and results only leave it if you export them.

What am I looking at? Each pilot has a live signal trace covering the last 20 seconds. The level is dB above a noise floor the timer tracks automatically. A fly-by should make a clear peak.

How is a pass detected? The signal must rise above Entry, then fall below Exit. A white marker shows an accepted pass. The timer uses the middle of the peak for its timestamp; the first pass starts your lap clock.

How do I tune it? Start with Medium sensitivity and fly past the gate. If passes are missed, try High. If it triggers too easily, try Low. For finer control, open the graph, expand Advanced and enable custom values, then type numbers or drag the Entry / Exit handles.

Keep Exit below Entry. The controls keep at least a 0.5 dB gap so signal noise does not repeatedly toggle the gate. Test your settings before starting: threshold editing is available during setup, while the graph can stay open during a session.
